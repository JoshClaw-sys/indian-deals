Three specs decide if a 43-inch TV is actually worth buying at this price: true 4K resolution (not 1080p), HDR support (Dolby Vision or HDR10+), and 24W or louder speakers. Everything else is preference.

We compared 12 TVs from Xiaomi, Realme, Kodak, TCL, Candes and Weston available on Amazon India and Flipkart in this band. The interesting finding: four of them are 1080p disguised as 'HD ready' — and that's the first trap to avoid when buying under ₹25,000.

If you only buy one thing from this list: get the Xiaomi TV A Pro 43. It is the only set in this band with both Dolby Vision and 30W Dolby Audio, paired with a Google TV stack that ships the full Play Store. At ₹23,499 on sale, it sits ₹2,000 below the next-best Dolby Vision 43-inch and beats everything in this segment on sound.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 43-inch TV under ₹25,000 for Netflix?
The Xiaomi TV A Pro 43 is the strongest pick because it ships Dolby Vision, which Netflix prefers over HDR10+. The Kodak 43UHDXMRT is a cheaper alternative at ₹20,000 but loses Dolby Vision.

Should I buy a 43-inch or 50-inch TV under ₹25,000?
43-inch. At ₹25,000, the 50-inch options are all 1080p or have weaker HDR. A 43-inch 4K panel at ₹24,000 looks sharper than a 50-inch 1080p at the same price. If you have a 16 m²+ room, stretch to ₹30,000 for a 50-inch 4K instead.

Can I use a 43-inch TV as a PC monitor?
Yes, but only for casual use. The 4K resolution makes text sharp, but the 60 Hz refresh rate and input lag (around 15 ms on these TVs) feel sluggish for first-person shooters or competitive games. For browsing, spreadsheets, and streaming, the Xiaomi and Kodak work well at 1.5 m viewing distance.

How much electricity does a 43-inch TV use?
A 43-inch 4K TV draws 60–90W under typical viewing — about 0.06–0.09 units per hour, or ₹3–₹6 for 8 hours of daily use on a standard Indian tariff plan. Standby is under 0.5W, so leaving it plugged in costs almost nothing.

Why the 43-inch size is the sweet spot for Indian bedrooms

Walk into any builder-floor flat in Bengaluru, Mumbai or Delhi NCR and you will find most bedrooms measure 10 to 16 square metres. A 43-inch TV is the largest panel that fits comfortably at 4 to 7 feet viewing distance without forcing you to crank your neck or sit off-axis. A 50-inch panel works in a living room but dominates a bedroom; a 32-inch starts to feel cramped once you move past 5 feet. The 43-inch size is the right compromise, which is why it sells more units than every other screen size combined in India -- around 38 percent of all TVs sold in 2025 were 43-inch, according to Counterpoint Research.

There is also a service angle most buyers miss. 43-inch panels are the most-repaired size in India, which means spare parts and authorised service centres are denser than for 50-inch or 55-inch sets. Xiaomi, Realme, Kodak and TCL all run company-owned service networks that cover 18,000 plus pin codes for 43-inch panels, versus 8,000 to 12,000 for larger sizes. If your panel fails in year three, a 43-inch TV from one of these four brands is more likely to be repaired under warranty than a comparable 55-inch set from the same brand.

What about 75-inch TVs that cost less than 50-inch used to?

You will see 75-inch TVs advertised under Rs 40,000, and it is tempting to size up. The catch is that most of those 75-inch panels are VA-type with 250 to 300 nits peak brightness and basic HDR10 support -- they look dim in a sunny Indian room and the smart TV platform is often a stripped-down Linux build. For Rs 25,000 specifically, the 43-inch category gives you Dolby Vision, Google TV, and 30W speakers. The 75-inch options in this band give you size but cut every other corner. Save the size upgrade for when your budget crosses Rs 50,000.

TV placement and HDMI cables that actually matter

Once your new TV arrives, the placement and cabling make more difference to your viewing experience than the panel itself. Mount the centre of the screen at eye level when seated -- for a 43-inch TV that is around 95 to 105 cm from the floor in a typical Indian bed or sofa. Avoid placing the panel directly opposite a window: glare washes out the picture, and you will end up closing curtains every afternoon. A north-facing wall is the ideal spot in most Indian homes.

For HDMI cables, spend Rs 350 to Rs 600 on a certified Ultra High Speed HDMI 2.1 cable from Amazon Basics, Belkin or Cable Matters. The cheap Rs 80 cables from local shops can drop 4K at 60 Hz signal and cause random flickering. You only need one for a streaming box or set-top box; the TV's built-in apps cover most use cases. If you plan to plug in a Fire TV Stick 4K or an Apple TV 4K, label the HDMI ports on the TV with painter's tape so the family stops fighting over which input to use.
