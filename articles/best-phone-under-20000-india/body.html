What actually matters in a phone under ₹20,000

Three years ago, ₹20,000 bought you a compromise. In 2026, it buys a genuinely great phone -- one that runs Genshin Impact at high settings, takes flagship-grade photos, and lasts a full day on a charge. But the field is crowded, and not every phone in this bracket is worth your money.

We picked six that are. Here's what we looked for, and what tripped up the one model we recommend skipping.

The four specs that decide if a ₹20,000 phone is actually good in 2026: chipset (Snapdragon 7-series or Dimensity 7000+), AMOLED 120Hz display, OIS on the main camera, 67W+ charging. Battery at 5000 mAh is now standard. Anything below 8 GB RAM in 2026 will feel sluggish within a year.

If you only buy one thing from this list: get the Redmi Note 14 Pro+ 5G. The 200 MP camera with OIS is genuinely flagship-grade, and the Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 chews through every app you'll throw at it.

Frequently asked questions

Is 5G worth it on a phone under ₹20,000?

Yes -- every phone in this list supports 5G, and Jio/Airtel 5G is now live in most metros. Hold on to a 5G phone for the next 3 years.

Which is better: AMOLED or LCD under ₹20,000?

AMOLED -- the colours, blacks, and in-display fingerprint readers on AMOLED are noticeably better. Every pick on this list uses AMOLED except the budget option.

How much RAM do I actually need?

8 GB minimum in 2026. 6 GB will work but you'll see apps reload when switching between heavy ones. All six picks here have 8 GB or more.

Should I wait for the next Redmi Note launch?

Not unless your current phone is broken. The Redmi Note 14 series is already in this list, and prices will drop ₹1,000-2,000 in October once stocks settle.

Bottom line

If you want the absolute best phone under ₹20,000 in India, buy the Redmi Note 14 Pro+ 5G. The 200 MP camera with OIS is genuinely flagship-grade, the 90W charging is the fastest in the bracket, and the Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 won't slow down for years. For a clean software experience, the Motorola G85 is the better pick. For long battery life, the Samsung Galaxy M35 is unbeatable. And if you just want the best value, the Poco X6 5G at ₹15,999 gives you 90% of the top pick's experience for ₹4,000 less.

Software updates and long-term ownership

The single biggest difference between a ₹20,000 phone in 2026 and one in 2023 is software support. Samsung's M35 ships with 4 years of OS updates and 5 years of security patches -- the best in the bracket. The Redmi Note 14 Pro+ and Poco X6 promise 3 years of OS updates. The Motorola G85 and iQOO Z9 get only 2 years, which means Android 16 will be their last major update.

If you tend to keep phones for 3+ years, the Samsung M35 is the safer long-term pick despite its slower charging. If you upgrade every 2 years, the difference doesn't matter -- pick on hardware.

5G bands and Indian carrier support

Every phone in this list supports the n78 band that Jio and Airtel 5G use, but band count varies. The Redmi Note 14 Pro+ supports 12 5G bands -- the most in the bracket -- so it will work on Vi 5G when it launches and on any future Indian carrier. The Realme 13 Pro+ and Poco X6 each support 9 bands. The Motorola G85 supports only 7 -- fine for Jio/Airtel today but may miss bands on international travel.

If you travel to the Middle East or Southeast Asia frequently, prioritise phones with more 5G bands. The Redmi Note 14 Pro+ and Samsung M35 are both excellent for this.

What to do with your old phone

If your current phone still works, don't throw it. Cashify and GreenDust will buy back a working 3-year-old phone for ₹2,000-4,000, which drops the effective price of any pick on this list significantly. The Redmi Note 14 Pro+ at ₹19,999 minus a ₹3,500 exchange for a Redmi Note 11 is a ₹16,499 phone with flagship cameras -- the best deal in the bracket right now.
