What actually matters in an air purifier under Rs 30,000

Skip the marketing. Three specs decide if an air purifier is worth buying in India: real-room CADR for PM 2.5 (not smoke), true H13 or H14 HEPA grade (not 'HEPA-type'), and annual filter replacement cost under Rs 4,000. Everything else -- app connectivity, voice control, mood lighting -- is incremental and rarely worth the premium in this bracket.

The Rs 18,000-30,000 segment is where Indian air purifier shopping gets serious. Below Rs 18,000 you are buying glorified fans with electrostatic filters that lose 40% efficiency in 6 months. Above Rs 30,000 you start paying for brand premiums (Dyson) or commercial-grade units you do not need at home. We evaluated five current models for this guide -- Sharp, Coway, Honeywell, Mi, and Dyson -- weighing clean-air delivery rate for actual Indian AQI conditions, medical-grade filter certification, noise on sleep mode, and the 5-year ownership cost (purifier + filters).

What most buyers underestimate: the CADR number on the box is usually measured for smoke particles, not PM 2.5. The smoke CADR can be 30-50% higher than the PM 2.5 CADR -- a purifier that says 500 m3/hr on the box may only deliver 300 m3/hr for the particles that actually hurt your lungs. Always look for the PM 2.5 CADR specifically, or a third-party test report. The picks below are based on Indian review lab measurements, not manufacturer claims.

One more consideration for Indian homes: the room size. The right CADR is room area (in sq ft) multiplied by 5. A 200 sq ft bedroom needs 400 m3/hr CADR for a full clean in 12-15 minutes. Smaller units clean slowly -- the air quality monitor might read 80 AQI but actually leave PM 2.5 elevated because the purifier cannot cycle the air fast enough. This is the single most common reason buyers think their purifier is broken.

If you only buy one thing from this list: get the Sharp FP-J80-W at Rs 27,990. 480 m3/hr PM 2.5 CADR handles a 400 sq ft room in 15 minutes, H14 medical-grade HEPA (the highest grade sold for residential use), and Sharp's Plasmacluster ion technology has independent 2024 testing showing 99% reduction in airborne virus particles. At Rs 40,000+ less than Dyson with better real-world performance, this is the obvious pick.

Frequently asked questions

What CADR do I need for a 200 sq ft bedroom?

For a 200 sq ft bedroom, you need at least 350-400 m3/hr CADR to clean the room in 12-15 minutes. Anything below 250 m3/hr will show good AQI on the built-in monitor but actually leave PM 2.5 elevated -- a common complaint with cheap purifiers. The picks above (Sharp, Coway, Honeywell, Mi) all exceed this threshold.

Do HEPA filters really remove PM 2.5?

True HEPA filters (H13 or H14 grade) remove 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns -- which includes PM 2.5, dust, pollen and most bacteria. Cheap purifiers labelled 'HEPA-type' or 'HEPA-style' are NOT the same and let 30-50% of PM 2.5 through. Always check for the H13/H14 grade on the spec sheet. The Sharp uses H14 (highest residential grade); Coway, Honeywell and Mi use H13.

How often do I need to replace the HEPA filter?

Genuine HEPA filters in this bracket last 12-18 months with daily 8-10 hour use in Indian AQI conditions (PM 2.5 above 150). Replacement filters cost Rs 2,500-4,500 per unit. The picks above all use standard-size filters so aftermarket options are available at 40-50% lower cost. Coway's filter warranty is the longest at 4 years; the others are 1-2 years on filters.

Are smart features and air quality monitors worth it?

Yes for the monitor (laser PM 2.5 sensor worth Rs 1,500+ standalone), no for the app. The monitor lets you verify the purifier is actually working -- if it shows green but you can see dust, the filter is saturated. The app is a nice-to-have but adds Rs 2,000-3,000 to the price for limited daily use. Coway, Sharp, Honeywell and Mi all include laser PM 2.5 sensors; Dyson does too but at 2.4x the price.

Can one air purifier handle the whole house?

No. A purifier only cleans the room it sits in -- air does not flow well between rooms. For a 3BHK flat, you need either 3 separate purifiers (one per bedroom + living room) or one large unit in the most-used room. The picks above cover bedrooms (200-300 sq ft) -- for living rooms above 350 sq ft, consider the Rs 35,000-50,000 tier like the Sharp FP-J100 or Coway Airmega 300.

The bottom line

The sub-Rs 30,000 air purifier market in India in 2026 has real options for every use case. The Sharp FP-J80-W at Rs 27,990 is the best overall pick -- 480 m3/hr PM 2.5 CADR handles up to 400 sq ft, H14 medical-grade HEPA removes 99.997% of particles, and the Plasmacluster ion technology has independent virus-reduction testing.

Light sleepers should pick the Coway Airmega 150 at Rs 24,500 for its 32 dB sleep mode and lowest annual filter cost. Buyers outside top-6 metros should pick the Honeywell Air Touch V2 at Rs 21,990 for the widest service network. And budget shoppers wanting the best CADR-per-rupee should pick the Mi Air Purifier 4 at Rs 18,500.

Skip the Dyson TP10 -- the Rs 68,000 price is brand premium, not performance premium. The PM 2.5 CADR is 33% lower than the Rs 27,990 Sharp, and the annual filter cost is the highest in this bracket. You can buy two Sharp FP-J80-W units (one for bedroom, one for living room) and a year's worth of filters for less than one Dyson TP10.

Two ownership tips that keep an air purifier working well: clean the pre-filter every 2 weeks (wash under running water, dry completely before reinstalling) -- this extends the HEPA filter life by 30-40% in dusty Indian conditions, and avoid placing the purifier in corners or against walls -- leave at least 1 foot of clearance on all sides for proper airflow.
