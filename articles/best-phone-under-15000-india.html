What actually matters in a phone under ₹15,000

The sub-₹15,000 band is the highest-volume slice of the Indian smartphone market and the one where specification sheets are least trustworthy. Every phone here advertises 5G, a 50MP camera and a 5,000mAh-plus battery. Those three numbers are close to identical across the band, which means they tell you almost nothing about which phone will still feel good in a year.

Four things actually separate these phones: panel technology, chipset process node, real charging wattage, and the published software update policy. Get those right and a ₹14,000 phone stays pleasant for three years. Get them wrong and you have a phone that dims in sunlight, throttles while gaming, takes two hours to charge, and stops receiving patches after eighteen months.

If you only buy one thing from this list: the POCO M7 Pro 5G. It is the only phone under ₹15,000 that pairs a 120Hz AMOLED panel with a 6nm chipset, 45W charging and OIS on the main camera — the four specifications that matter most, without a compromise on any of them.

Display: AMOLED first, refresh rate second

An AMOLED panel lights each pixel individually, so black is genuinely black, contrast is effectively infinite, and peak brightness in a sunlit window is far better than any LCD in this price band manages. It also enables an always-on display without meaningful battery cost. The practical difference is not subtle: side by side, an AMOLED phone looks like it costs ₹5,000 more.

Refresh rate is the secondary consideration. 120Hz makes scrolling smoother and is now common even on budget LCDs, which is precisely why it is a weak differentiator. Note also that some phones in this band pair 120Hz with only an HD+ (1600 x 720) resolution rather than full HD+ — the Redmi 14C is the notable example. On a 6.88-inch screen, HD+ is visibly soft on text.

Chipset: the process node tells you more than the name

Every chipset in this list is fast enough for messaging, streaming and casual gaming. What varies is efficiency, and that is governed by the manufacturing process. A 4nm or 6nm chip — Dimensity 7025 Ultra, Dimensity 7300, Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, Snapdragon 6 Gen 4, Exynos 1330 — does the same work with less heat and less battery drain than an older 12nm part with a comparable benchmark score.

This matters most under sustained load. A 12nm chip running a game for twenty minutes gets hot, throttles its clocks, and drains the battery quickly; the 4nm and 6nm parts here hold their frequencies far longer. When comparing two phones at the same price, check the fabrication node before the core count.

Battery and charging: capacity is table stakes, wattage is not

Every phone in this band ships with 5,000mAh or more, and two of the picks here go past 6,000mAh. Capacity is therefore not a differentiator — charging speed is. A 5,160mAh battery on an 18W charger needs roughly two hours for a full cycle. The same capacity on 45W is done in about an hour, and gets to 50 per cent in twenty-odd minutes.

Two details to verify on the listing. First, whether the fast charger is included in the box — most Indian variants still include it, but some do not. Second, the phone’s own supported wattage rather than the cable rating: a phone rated for 18W will not charge faster on a 65W brick.

The RAM is the fatal specification: WhatsApp, a browser and one more app is enough to force constant reloading, and no software update will fix it.

The gap to the Redmi 14C 5G is roughly ₹3,500–₹4,000, and it buys a 4nm 5G chipset, 4GB of RAM, a 120Hz panel and a far larger screen. We would also skip any phone in this band still shipping 4G only — 5G is standard under ₹12,000 now, and a 4G-only phone bought in 2026 will feel dated well before it wears out.

The bottom line

Buy the POCO M7 Pro 5G if you want the best phone under ₹15,000 with no significant weak point: a 120Hz full HD+ AMOLED panel, a 6nm chipset, 45W charging and OIS on the main camera. The Samsung Galaxy M17 5G is the pick if you intend to keep the phone four years or more, because its update commitment and service network are the strongest here.

On a tighter budget, the Redmi 14C 5G is the cheapest sensible 5G phone in India, provided you accept an HD+ LCD panel and 18W charging. The Realme P3 5G is the choice for heavy users who want 6,000mAh with AMOLED, and the iQOO Z10x 5G is the gaming pick if you will trade display quality for sustained frame rates. Frequently asked questions

Is 5G worth it under ₹15,000 in India?

Yes, and it is no longer a premium feature — every pick in this list supports 5G, and Jio and Airtel 5G coverage now reaches most tier-1 and tier-2 cities. What matters is band support: check that the phone covers n78 (3500MHz), which carries the bulk of Indian 5G traffic, and n28 (700MHz) for indoor and rural coverage. A phone listing many 5G bands but missing n78 will fall back to 4G in most places.

AMOLED or a higher refresh rate LCD — which should I prioritise?

AMOLED, in almost every case. A 120Hz LCD scrolls smoothly but still shows grey-black instead of true black, has lower contrast and is noticeably dimmer outdoors. AMOLED at 90Hz beats LCD at 120Hz for daily use because contrast and outdoor brightness affect every minute of use while the extra 30Hz only shows in fast scrolling. The POCO M7 Pro and Realme P3 give you both AMOLED and 120Hz, which is why they lead this list.

How many software updates will I get on a ₹15,000 phone?

Two major OS updates plus three years of security patches is the best on offer in this band, and Samsung publishes the strongest commitment of the brands here. POCO and Redmi typically promise two OS updates and three to four years of security patches. Realme and iQOO are usually similar on paper but historically slower to deliver. Treat the published policy as a ceiling, not a guarantee, and factor it in if you plan to keep the phone beyond three years.

Is 6GB RAM enough, or should I pay for 8GB?

6GB is workable for browsing, messaging, streaming and casual games, which is what most buyers in this band actually do. The ₹2,000 step to 8GB mainly buys you more apps staying resident in the background rather than reloading. If your budget is tight, prefer the 6GB variant of a better phone over the 8GB variant of a weaker one — the chipset and panel matter more. Ignore any listing that advertises 'virtual RAM' or 'extended RAM' as real memory; it is swap on the storage chip and much slower.

Do these phones have OIS for photos and video?

Only some. The POCO M7 Pro and Samsung Galaxy M17 include optical image stabilisation on the main sensor, which visibly helps low-light stills and handheld video. The Redmi 14C, Realme P3 and iQOO Z10x rely on electronic stabilisation, which crops the frame and cannot rescue a genuinely dark scene. If you shoot a lot of video or evening photos, treat OIS as the single most valuable camera feature in this band — far more so than the secondary 2MP macro sensors most of these phones list.
