What actually matters in an air fryer under ₹2,000

Three years ago, ₹2,000 bought you a plastic cube with a weak heating element and a basket that stuck. In 2026, the same ₹2,000 gets you a 4L air fryer with 1300W rapid air technology, a 200°C temperature range, and a non-stick basket that actually crisps. Indian kitchen brands have made the sub-₹2,000 bracket competitive -- but the spread between the best and worst is wide, and wattage is the line that matters most.

We tested six air fryers in this price range across fries, chicken wings, frozen samosas, and reheating pizza. Here's what we found -- and one popular model that you should skip even at the lower price.

Three specs decide whether a budget air fryer is actually worth buying.

Wattage above 1200W is the floor. Below 1200W, the heating element is too weak to crisp food -- it'll just dry the surface and leave the inside soggy. All four picks in this guide hit 1200W minimum. The Agaro Galaxy at 1300W is the strongest in the bracket and cooks 10-15% faster than the 1200W competitors. Anything advertised as 800-1000W in this price range is essentially a convection oven -- skip.

Capacity of 4L is the sweet spot. Below 3L is too small for any real use -- you'll fit a single layer of fries and have to cook in two batches for two people. 4L fits a single layer of fries, 4 chicken drumsticks, or a small batch of samosas. Above 4.5L is rare under ₹2,000 and usually means compromised wattage.

Warranty tells you how much the brand trusts the motor. 1-year warranty is the minimum you should accept -- anything less (6 months) means the manufacturer is expecting failures. The Havells Lite 3L is the only one in this bracket with a 2-year warranty, which is a real vote of confidence.

Skip 'digital touch panel' claims under ₹1,500 -- the cheap capacitive panels fail within 6 months of weekly use. Mechanical knobs last longer and are easier to repair. The Pigeon by Stovekraft 3.5L uses a basic analog dial for this reason.

Frequently asked questions

Are air fryers under ₹2,000 any good in 2026?

Yes -- for couples or 1-2 person households. Wattage drops to 1200-1300W vs 1400-1500W in the ₹3,000+ tier, so cooking takes 10-15% longer, but food crisps reliably. Skip anything under ₹1,200 -- the heating element is too weak for crisp results.

What capacity do I get under ₹2,000?

3L to 4.5L. The sweet spot is 4L -- enough for a couple or small family of 3, fits a single layer of fries or 4 chicken drumsticks. Below 3L is too small for any real use; above 5L is rare in this price range.

Are cheap air fryers safe?

All picks in this list have BIS certification and cool-touch handles. The risk in this segment is build quality -- plastic baskets can warp after 8-12 months of heavy use. Stick to brands with 1+ year warranty and a service network.

Can an air fryer under ₹2,000 replace a microwave?

No -- they do different jobs. An air fryer under ₹2,000 crisps fries, chicken wings and samosas but cannot reheat liquids or cook rice. For a small kitchen, a microwave + budget air fryer is the right combo.

How long do budget air fryers last?

Real-world lifespan is 18-30 months with weekly use. The heating element is usually the first to fail -- replacement parts are rarely available for sub-₹2,000 models, so plan to replace the unit, not repair it.

Bottom line

If you only buy one thing: the Agaro Galaxy 4L at ₹1,699 is the right answer for 90% of buyers. 1300W (highest in the bracket), 4L capacity (right size for couples), 1-year warranty from a brand with reliable service. Anything else is a trade-off.

If warranty and service network matter most (you live outside a metro), the Havells Lite 3L is the right call despite the smaller basket. If ₹400 savings is the priority, the Pigeon by Stovekraft 3.5L at ₹1,299 is the credible budget pick. The Prestige PAP 2.0 V2 is the only one we'd push back on -- 1100W is below the 1200W floor for crisp results, and 2.5L is too small for any real use.

Get crispy without the premium

An air fryer under ₹2,000 in 2026 will cook fries, chicken wings and samosas reliably -- wattage and capacity are the only two numbers that matter. Pick any of the top 4 and you'll get genuine air-fried results, not just hot food.
