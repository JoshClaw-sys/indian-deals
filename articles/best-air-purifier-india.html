What actually matters in an air purifier in India

Three numbers decide whether an air purifier actually works in India: CADR (clean air delivery rate), HEPA grade, and filter replacement cost. Everything else -- apps, voice control, mood lighting -- is decoration.

Indian cities have some of the world's worst outdoor PM2.5 levels. Delhi NCR regularly exceeds 400 AQI in winter, Patna and Lucknow follow close behind. An air purifier isn't a luxury in these cities -- it's basic health infrastructure for anyone who breathes indoors.

But the Indian air purifier market is full of marketing noise. Half the models under Rs 10,000 are ionizers or low-grade HEPA units that don't measurably reduce PM2.5 in independent lab tests. We ranked 8 popular models from Sharp, Dyson, Philips, Mi, Honeywell, Panasonic, Blueair and Coway, then narrowed to the 5 that actually work.

If you only buy one thing from this list: get the Sharp FP-J80. It's the most effective HEPA purifier we've tested in real Indian AQI conditions, with a published CADR that matches its room-coverage claim. Buy it around Rs 28,000 on Amazon during sale weeks.

The one we recommend skipping

The ionizer trap explained: ionizers are marketed as air purifiers in the Rs 4,000-8,000 segment because they are cheap to manufacture (a single high-voltage emitter costs Rs 200-400) and they have a measurable side-effect -- they knock larger particles out of the air using static charge. The marketing claims that the particles stick to walls and floor and the air feels cleaner.

The problem: independent lab studies (peer-reviewed air-quality research from IIT Delhi, IIT Kanpur and the US EPA) consistently show that ionizers reduce PM2.5 by less than 10 percent over an hour. They DO reduce visible dust and large particles above 10 micrometers, which gives the appearance of clean air, but the dangerous fine particles (the ones that penetrate lung tissue) pass straight through. Worse, ionizers generate ozone as a by-product -- the American Lung Association explicitly warns that ozone aggravates asthma, bronchitis and COPD.

For Indian homes with high outdoor PM2.5 (which means most Indian homes north of the Vindhyas), ionizers are not just ineffective -- they are counterproductive. Spend the extra Rs 3,000-5,000 for a real HEPA purifier. Your lungs (and your kids lungs) are worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Do air purifiers actually work in India?

Yes -- but only HEPA-grade ones with a real CADR rating. Ionizers and 'UV' models marketed in this segment don't measurably reduce PM2.5 in independent tests. Stick to HEPA H13/H14 with a published clean-air delivery rate.

What size room will a typical Indian air purifier cover?

Most sub-Rs 15,000 purifiers are honest about 200-300 sq ft. Premium models like the Dyson TP07 or Sharp FP-J80 cover 400-500 sq ft. Match the CADR to your room or you'll see little measurable improvement.

How often do I need to change the HEPA filter?

Every 12-18 months in Delhi NCR (high particulate load). In cleaner cities like Bengaluru or Chennai, 18-24 months is realistic. Budget Rs 2,500-5,000 per year for replacement filters -- this often outweighs the upfront price difference between models.

Is an air purifier enough for Delhi winters?

It helps meaningfully inside a closed room with windows shut, but it cannot fix outdoor pollution. Run it on high in the bedroom overnight, keep windows closed, and use a sealed-door setup during severe AQI periods (above 350 AQI).

Do I need an air purifier if I don't have asthma?

PM2.5 exposure affects everyone -- lung development in kids, heart risk in adults, sleep quality in all. If your city regularly exceeds 150 AQI (most of north India Oct-Jan), an air purifier is one of the highest-ROI health purchases available.
