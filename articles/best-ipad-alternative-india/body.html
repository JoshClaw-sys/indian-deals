What actually matters in an iPad alternative in 2026

The 'best iPad alternative' question in India in 2026 has a real answer for the first time. Through 2018–2022, Android tablets trailed the iPad by 2–3 years on chipset performance and the gap on creator apps was 5+ years. In 2024–2025, the Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 and 8 Gen 3 chipsets closed the performance gap, and OxygenOS for Pad / One UI for Tablet finally delivered tablet-specific Android experiences. The remaining gap is app ecosystem, and that's narrowing — but not closed.

The three things that actually move the needle on an iPad alternative in 2026 are chipset single-core performance, stylus ecosystem, and creator app availability. Display quality matters too, but every contender in this price band now ships a 120 Hz or 144 Hz panel with proper HDR support — the days of 60 Hz Android flagships are over.

If you only buy one thing from this list: the OnePlus Pad 2. The Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 chipset matches the M2 iPad Air on single-core benchmarks, the 12.1-inch 144 Hz display is sharper and smoother than the iPad Air's 60 Hz panel, the 9510 mAh battery gives 12+ hours of mixed use, and OxygenOS for Pad handles split-screen multitasking better than any other Android skin. Pair it with the OnePlus Stylo 2 (₹5,500) and a Bluetooth keyboard and you have a credible iPad Pro replacement for ₹45,000 all-in.

Chipset: Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 is the new floor

The chipset is the single biggest predictor of how long a premium tablet stays fast. The Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 in the OnePlus Pad 2 is flagship-class silicon that will handle every Android app released through 2028 without compromise. The Snapdragon 8s Gen 3 in the Xiaomi Pad 7 Pro is a step down but still 2–3 years ahead of mid-range Android tablets. The Snapdragon 870 in the Lenovo Tab P12 Pro is older silicon — it was flagship-class in 2021 and remains fast for daily tasks but starts showing slowdowns around the 3-year mark. Avoid anything with Snapdragon 7-series or lower at this price tier.

Stylus: Samsung's S Pen sets the bar

Stylus support is the most differentiated feature in this category. Samsung's S Pen (included with the Tab S10 FE) is the best Android stylus experience — 4096 pressure levels, palm rejection, no charging needed, and magnetic attachment to the tablet's back. OnePlus's Stylo 2 supports pressure levels and palm rejection but requires USB-C charging and costs ~₹5,500 separately. Xiaomi Smart Pen supports pressure but the latency is visibly higher than Samsung or OnePlus. For sketchers and handwritten note-takers, Samsung's bundled S Pen is the decisive feature.

App ecosystem: the honest gap

The iPad's app ecosystem is still 2–3 years ahead on pro creator apps. Procreate, LumaFusion, Affinity Designer, and the full Final Cut Pro experience remain iPad-only. Android's equivalents — Concepts, Krita, Clip Studio Paint, LumaFusion (Android version, 2025 launch) — are catching up but not yet at parity. For reading, video, note-taking, and general productivity, the OnePlus Pad 2 and Samsung Tab S10 FE genuinely match the iPad. For serious creative work, iPadOS is still the right answer.

Display: 144 Hz is the new flagship baseline

Every tablet in this guide runs at 120 Hz or higher. The OnePlus Pad 2 and Xiaomi Pad 7 Pro hit 144 Hz which is overkill for most content but noticeable when scrolling long documents or playing 144 Hz mobile games. The Lenovo Tab P12 Pro's 12.6-inch AMOLED panel has the deepest blacks of any tablet here — worth the premium if you watch a lot of HDR content. The Samsung Tab S10 FE's 90 Hz LCD is the slowest refresh rate in this guide but is fine for productivity work.

Frequently asked questions

Is an iPad alternative really as good as an iPad?

For most buyers — yes. The chipset gap closed in 2024–2025 and the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 in the OnePlus Pad 2 matches the M2 in the iPad Air on Geekbench single-core. The remaining gap is the app ecosystem: Procreate, LumaFusion, Affinity Designer, and the full Final Cut Pro experience remain iPad-only. For reading, video, note-taking, and general Android apps, the OnePlus Pad 2 and Samsung Tab S10 FE genuinely rival the iPad Air.

Do I need a stylus, and which tablet has the best one?

Only if you sketch or take handwritten notes. For pure typing and reading, a ₹1,500 Bluetooth keyboard and a finger are enough. Samsung's S Pen (built into the Tab S10 FE) is the best stylus experience — 4096 pressure levels, palm rejection, no charging needed. OnePlus Stylo 2 costs ~₹5,500 extra and charges over USB-C. Xiaomi Smart Pen costs ~₹4,500 and has lower pressure-level accuracy.

How long will an Android tablet stay fast?

The Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 and 8s Gen 3 chipsets in the OnePlus Pad 2 and Xiaomi Pad 7 Pro are flagship-class — expect 4+ years of smooth performance. The Snapdragon 870 in the Lenovo Tab P12 Pro is older (2021) but still fast; you'll see slowdowns around the 3-year mark. Samsung promises 7 Android updates on the Tab S10 FE, which is the longest software support commitment in the Android tablet market.

Can I use these tablets for work and productivity?

Yes for email, documents, video calls, and note-taking. The OnePlus Pad 2 and Samsung Tab S10 FE both have multi-window Android implementations that handle 2–3 apps side-by-side. For pro apps (Photoshop, Premiere Pro, full IDE), even the iPad falls short — a ₹60,000 laptop is the real productivity answer. For 'good enough' work tablet duties, the OnePlus Pad 2 with a Bluetooth keyboard covers 80% of use cases.

Is LTE worth it on a premium tablet?

Only if you genuinely hotspot on the move without your phone. The Samsung Tab S10 FE and Xiaomi Pad 7 Pro both offer LTE variants at a ₹3,000–₹5,000 premium. For everyone else, phone-tethering handles the on-the-go case at zero extra hardware cost. Most buyers should skip the LTE premium.

Bottom line

The OnePlus Pad 2 at ₹36,999–₹42,999 is the best iPad alternative in India for most buyers — fastest chipset, cleanest Android skin, brightest display. The Samsung Tab S10 FE is the pick if you want a stylus in the box and 7 years of software support. The Xiaomi Pad 7 Pro is the right pick at ₹32,999 if you want flagship performance on a tighter budget. Skip the Realme Pad 5G — the Snapdragon 695 chipset is the wrong fit for this category.
