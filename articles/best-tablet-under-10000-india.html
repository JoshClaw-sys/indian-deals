What actually matters in a tablet under ₹10,000

A budget tablet is one of the most useful study and entertainment devices in an Indian household in 2026 — for online classes, reading, and streaming. In this price band, the wrong choice is mostly picking a 3GB-RAM tablet with a 32GB storage and an old Android version — both of which you only notice 6 months in, when the tablet starts stuttering on app updates.

The four things that actually move the needle on a sub-₹10,000 Indian tablet in 2026 are RAM, storage, screen size, and Android version. Battery life matters too, but most tablets in this band have 5,000-6,650 mAh which is enough for a full day of mixed use.

If you only buy one thing from this list: the Redmi Pad SE 8.7" (Helio G85, 4GB/64GB). It has the 90Hz HD+ screen, Helio G85 chip, 4GB RAM, 64GB storage, 6,650 mAh battery, and Xiaomi's widest service network. The 3.5mm headphone jack is also still present — useful for wired earphones during online classes.

RAM: 4GB is the floor for Android 14+

Android 14 and 15 use more RAM than Android 12. A 3GB-RAM tablet stutters when you switch between 3-4 apps (browser + WhatsApp + YouTube + a notes app), which is the typical study / class workflow. 4GB is comfortable for this; 6GB or more only matters for gaming or split-screen multitasking, which budget tablets don't handle well anyway. All tablets in this list have 4GB or more, except the skip pick.

Storage: 64GB is the floor; 32GB fills up fast

Android 14 itself takes about 12-15GB; add WhatsApp (2-3GB), YouTube (1-2GB), a notes app, and a streaming app, and you're already at 20-25GB on a 32GB tablet. The remaining 5-7GB fills up within a year of downloads and offline videos. 64GB is the right floor; all tablets in this list (except the skip pick) have 64GB. Most also have a microSD slot for up to 1TB expansion.

Screen size: 8-inch is the sweet spot

8-inch is the sweet spot for one-hand reading, online classes, and commuting. 10-inch is better for split-screen multitasking and watching video with two people, but bulkier for one-hand use. Below 8-inch, the screen feels cramped for reading PDFs and textbooks. Above 10-inch, the tablet is heavy for long reading sessions. The Redmi Pad SE and Samsung Galaxy Tab A9 are 8.7-inch; the Lenovo Tab M9 is 9.0-inch — all in the right range.

Android version: Android 14+ with 2 years of updates

Android 14 is the right baseline in 2026 — Android 12 tablets are already one version behind and miss out on security updates. Samsung promises 4 years of OS updates on the Galaxy Tab A series (the longest in this price band); Redmi promises 2 years; Lenovo promises 2 years. For a tablet you'll use 3+ years, longer update support matters.

Frequently asked questions

Is 4GB RAM enough on a budget tablet?

Yes for reading, streaming, and online classes — the daily-use apps that most budget tablet buyers use. 3GB RAM stutters on Android 14+ when you switch between 3-4 apps; 4GB is comfortable. Above 4GB only matters for gaming or split-screen multitasking, which budget tablets don't handle well anyway.

Do I need 4G SIM on a budget tablet?

Only if you'll use the tablet outside Wi-Fi regularly — commuting, travel, or outdoor study. For home use, Wi-Fi only is fine and saves ₹1,500-₹2,500. Most budget tablets have a Wi-Fi-only variant and a 4G variant at the same screen size; check the listing carefully.

8-inch vs 10-inch: which is right?

8-inch is the sweet spot for one-hand reading, online classes, and commuting. 10-inch is better for split-screen multitasking, sketching, and watching video with two people. Below 8-inch, the screen feels cramped for reading PDFs and textbooks. Above 10-inch, the tablet is bulky for one-hand use.

How long does a budget tablet's battery last?

Most budget tablets in this price band have a 5,000-6,650 mAh battery, which translates to 7-10 hours of mixed use (reading + streaming + light apps). The Redmi Pad SE leads with 6,650 mAh; the Lenovo Tab M9 trails at 5,100 mAh. For comparison, an iPad Mini lasts about 10 hours, but costs 4-5x as much.

Can I use a budget tablet for online classes?

Yes — 8-inch budget tablets handle Zoom / Google Meet / Teams classes fine. Look for a front camera of 5MP or higher (most have 5MP or 8MP), a microphone array that cancels background noise, and Wi-Fi 5 (ac) or later for stable video. The Samsung Galaxy Tab A9 has the best front camera in this list; the Redmi Pad SE has the best battery for long class sessions.
