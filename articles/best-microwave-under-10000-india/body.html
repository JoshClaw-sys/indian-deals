What actually matters in a microwave under Rs 10,000

Three numbers decide whether a microwave under Rs 10,000 actually works in an Indian kitchen: capacity, output wattage, and mode (solo/grill/convection). Everything else -- presets, child lock, display color -- is decoration.

The under-Rs 10,000 microwave market in India is dominated by LG, IFB, Samsung, Bajaj, Morphy Richards and Panasonic. There are 200+ models in this segment on Amazon India alone, and most of them are differentiated by feature lists rather than cooking performance. After ranking 8 of the strongest models, the choice comes down to how you actually use the microwave: reheating only, daily Indian cooking, or occasional baking.

For most Indian kitchens, a 20-25L microwave with 700-900W output and grill mode under Rs 10,000 is the right pick. The 4 picks below match this shape.

  • Capacity -- 20L for 1-2 person households. 23-25L for 3-4 person families (most Indian homes). Below 20L, the turntable is too small for a dinner plate. Above 30L, you pay 30-40 percent more for capacity you do not need.
  • Output wattage -- 700W minimum (LG MS2043DB sits at this floor). 800-900W is the sweet spot for fast reheating. Below 700W, reheating takes 50 percent longer -- frustrating when you are heating lunch in 90 seconds versus 3 minutes.
  • Mode (solo vs grill vs convection) -- Solo for reheating, defrosting, simple cooking. Grill adds grilling (paneer tikka, kebabs). Convection adds baking (cakes, pizzas, tandoori). For most Indian kitchens, grill is the right pick -- convection is only worth it if you bake weekly.
  • Magnetron warranty -- The magnetron is the heart of the microwave. 3-4 year magnetron warranty (LG, Panasonic) signals confidence in long-term reliability. 1-year product warranty + 1-year magnetron is the floor.
If you only buy one thing from this list: the LG MS2043DB Solo Microwave. 700W output, 20L capacity, i-Wave technology (no cold spots in dal), anti-bacterial cavity, and a 4-year magnetron warranty. Rs 6,490 is not the cheapest 20L solo, but LG service in 500+ Indian cities and the magnetron warranty make it the lowest total-cost-of-ownership in this segment.

Solo vs grill vs convection: which to buy

Indian microwave buyers often get confused between the three modes because the price difference is small but the use cases are very different.

Solo microwaves (Rs 5,000-8,000) do three things: reheat, defrost, and cook simple dishes (dal, rice, sabzi, idli). The heating is uniform if the microwave has i-Wave or inverter technology. They cannot grill or bake. For bachelors, students, and small families who only reheat and cook basic Indian food, a solo microwave is the right pick.

Grill microwaves (Rs 7,000-12,000) add a heating element that lets you grill paneer tikka, kebabs, bread pizza, and toast sandwiches. They also handle all solo-mode tasks. For Indian kitchens, grill is the sweet spot -- you get solo + grilling without paying for convection features you may not use.

Convection microwaves (Rs 9,000-25,000) add a fan-forced heating element that bakes cakes, pizzas, cookies, and tandoori dishes. The cheap end (under Rs 12,000) has weak convection fans and uneven baking -- the convection mode is mostly a marketing checkbox. The mid-range (Rs 15,000-25,000, like LG MC2886BRUM or IFB 30BRC2) actually bakes well. Stretch to this band if you bake weekly.

For most Indian kitchens, grill mode at Rs 7,000-10,000 is the right pick. Convection under Rs 10,000 is mostly a compromise.

Frequently asked questions

What size microwave do I need for an Indian kitchen?

20L for 1-2 person households (reheating, simple cooking). 23-25L for 3-4 person families (most Indian homes -- this is the sweet spot). 28-32L for 5+ person families or if you cook large batches of rice/dal. Below 20L, the turntable is too small for a dinner plate. Above 30L, you pay 30-40 percent more for capacity most Indian kitchens do not need.

Solo vs grill vs convection -- which to buy?

Solo: reheating, defrosting, simple cooking (dal, rice, basic curries). Rs 5,000-8,000. Grill: solo + grilling (paneer tikka, kebabs, bread pizza). Rs 7,000-12,000. Convection: solo + grill + baking (cakes, pizzas, tandoori). Rs 9,000-18,000. For most Indian kitchens, grill (Rs 7,000-10,000) is the right pick -- convection is only worth it if you bake at least twice a month.

How much electricity does a microwave use?

A 20-25L solo microwave uses 800-900W on high (about 0.8-0.9 units per hour of cooking). Reheating 3-4 rotis + dal + sabzi for a family of 4 uses about 0.3-0.4 units (Rs 2-3). Grill and convection modes use 50-100 percent more electricity because they heat the heating element + magnetron. Inverter microwaves (LG, Panasonic, Samsung premium) cut this by 20-30 percent.

What is a microwave inverter and is it worth the price?

Inverter technology varies the magnetron power continuously instead of cycling on/off at full power. The result: more even cooking (no cold spots in dal), faster reheating (15-20 percent less time), and 20-30 percent less electricity. Inverter microwaves cost Rs 1,500-3,000 more than non-inverter equivalents and are worth it if you cook daily. Inverter is rare under Rs 10,000 -- most picks below are non-inverter.

Is a convection microwave under Rs 10,000 worth buying?

Marginal. The convection microwave ovens under Rs 10,000 (e.g. IFB 20SC2, Morphy Richards 25CG) cut corners on heating element quality and fan speed. They bake unevenly and the convection fan makes more noise than Rs 15,000+ models. If you bake weekly, stretch to Rs 12,000-15,000 for an LG MC2886BRUM or IFB 30BRC2. If you only want convection for occasional pizza and cake, a Rs 9,000 convection is fine.
