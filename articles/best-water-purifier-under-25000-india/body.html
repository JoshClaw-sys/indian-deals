What actually matters in a water purifier under Rs 25,000

Skip the marketing. Three specs decide if a water purifier is worth buying: RO+UV+MTDS stage count, tank size (7L for a family of 4), and service network coverage. Everything else -- copper cartridges, alkaline stages, app connectivity -- is incremental improvement that matters less than the basics working reliably.

The Rs 12,000-25,000 bracket is where Indian purifier brands make their money, and the competition in 2026 is fierce. Eureka Forbes (Aquaguard), HUL (Pureit), Kent, and Blue Star are the established players; Livpure and A.O. Smith compete aggressively on price. We compared five current models for this guide, weighing TDS handling with and without mineralizer stages, tank size for typical family use, filter replacement cost, and the practical reality of service network turnaround time.

What most buyers underestimate: the cost of filter replacements adds Rs 2,500-4,000 per year on top of the initial price. Purifiers with annual maintenance contracts (AMC) often work out cheaper than pay-as-you-go, and the brands with the widest service networks can usually send a technician within 24-48 hours -- critical when your only source of drinking water is a purifier that stops working on a Sunday night.

One more consideration for Indian homes: input water TDS. Municipal water in metros is usually 200-500 ppm (TDS below 200 needs only UV, not RO; TDS above 1,500 from borewell needs a more aggressive membrane). The picks below assume municipal supply in the 200-800 ppm range -- which covers 80%+ of Indian households.

If you only buy one thing from this list: get the Eureka Forbes Aquaguard Aura RO+UV+MTDS at Rs 23,990. 7-litre tank, mineralizer stage for taste, and Aquaguard has 600+ service centres across India -- the widest network of any purifier brand. If the filter ever fails, a technician is on your doorstep within 24 hours.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Rs 20,000 water purifier really better than a Rs 10,000 one?

Yes, in three measurable ways: TDS adjustment (mineralizer adds back calcium and magnesium that RO strips out), filter life (premium filters last 12 months vs 6 on budget models), and tank build quality (food-grade steel vs plastic on cheap units). The cheap ones work, but taste flatter and filter replacements add up faster.

Do I need UV if I have RO?

For municipal tap water in Indian metros, RO alone is sufficient. UV adds protection against bacteria and viruses in case the RO membrane is aging or feed water is contaminated. For borewell water or tanker-supplied water, both RO and UV are essential.

How much do water purifier filters cost annually?

Budget purifiers cost Rs 1,500-2,500 per year in filters. Premium purifiers like the picks below average Rs 1,800-2,200 per year -- similar total cost but with better membrane life. The Aquaguard and Pureit service networks include filter cost in annual maintenance contracts (AMC) for Rs 2,500-4,000 per year.

What TDS level is safe to drink after RO?

RO strips TDS down to 30-50 ppm, which is too low for taste and health (the WHO recommends 100-300 ppm). Premium purifiers add a mineralizer stage to bring TDS back to 150-200 ppm. If you drink RO water straight, add a pinch of pink salt or use a mineralizer cartridge.

Can a water purifier work without electricity?

No. RO+UV purifiers need power to run the pump and UV lamp. The picks below all have built-in tanks so you can still drink during power cuts (6-12 hours of storage). For areas with frequent long outages, consider a non-electric gravity-based purifier as a backup.

Two ownership tips that keep a water purifier healthy: replace the sediment and carbon filters every 6 months even if the purifier says they are fine (the saturation is gradual and you will not notice the drop until year 2), and clean the storage tank every 6 months by emptying it, refilling with clean water, and discarding the first 2 litres after refill.
