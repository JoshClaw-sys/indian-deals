What actually matters in an RO water purifier in 2026

Choosing an RO water purifier in India used to be a brand question — Kent vs Pureit vs Aquaguard — and not much else. In 2026, the brands still matter (service network is the single biggest variable in real-world satisfaction), but the specifications have moved enough that the wrong pick can burn 25–30% of your water and leave you with tasteless output.

The three things that actually move the needle on an Indian household RO in 2026 are recovery rate, post-treatment, and service network reach. Litre capacity and tank size matter too, but the bigger 12 L tanks from sub-brands usually come with weaker membranes and lower recovery rates — you get more water but waste more of it.

If you only buy one thing from this list: the Kent Grand Plus (11016). Its 75% recovery rate is the best in this price band, the 9 L tank suits a family of 4–5, the UV+UF post-treatment handles municipal supply well, and Kent's service network is the widest in India with a 90-minute city SLA in most metros.

Recovery rate: the underrated water-saving spec

Recovery rate is the percentage of input water that becomes purified water. A 75% recovery RO turns 4 L of input into 3 L of purified water and wastes 1 L. A 50% recovery RO turns 4 L into 2 L and wastes 2 L. In water-scarce cities — Chennai, Bengaluru, parts of Mumbai — the higher recovery rate is worth a small premium. The Kent Grand Plus, LG Puricare, and a few newer Pureit models sit at 75%; most Aquaguard models and the older Eureka Forbes units sit at 55–60%. The difference compounds over a year: a 75% recovery RO in a 4-person household wastes ~120 L less per year than a 55% recovery unit.

Post-RO treatment: UV, UF, copper, mineral cartridges

RO strips out everything — bacteria, viruses, heavy metals, and the minerals that make water taste good. Post-RO treatment rebuilds some of that. UV disinfects the purified water and matters most for municipal supply where there's a real (if small) chance of post-RO contamination in the tank. UF is a secondary physical filter that catches any carbon or membrane fines. Copper cartridges (Pureit Copper+ Mineral) infuse copper ions into the water, which is a real thing in ayurvedic practice but used for taste and marketing more than verified health benefit. Mineral cartridges add calcium and magnesium back to bring the TDS up to a more drinkable 50–100 ppm range.

Service network beats specifications on paper

An RO purifier is one of the few home appliances that needs a service visit every 6–12 months to swap filters and sanitise the tank. A brand with great specs and a weak service network — almost every sub-₹8,000 marketplace RO — will frustrate you after the first year. Kent, Pureit (HUL), Eureka Forbes, and LG all have wide service networks in tier-1 and tier-2 cities. Aquaguard (Eureka Forbes) is wider in some South Indian cities; Kent is wider in the North and West; Pureit is comparable across metros. For tier-3 and rural buyers, the gap is bigger — Pureit and Aquaguard are usually the most reliable.

The one we recommend skipping

⚠️ Skip

Sub-₹6,000 no-name RO purifiers from marketplace listings

Storage5–7 L tank
StagesRO only
RecoveryUnknown
NotesNo service network

Marketplace listings under ₹6,000 that promise RO+UV+UF with a 7 L tank and TDS controller are almost always built on Shenzhen reference designs. The membrane is smaller and lower-rejection than the membrane in a Kent or Pureit, the recovery rate is usually 30–40% (you waste 2 L of water for every 1 L purified), and the manufacturer disappears after the first 6 months — leaving you with no service network when the UV lamp dies or the membrane clogs.

We also recommend skipping any RO purifier that doesn't disclose the membrane brand (a real Dow / Vontron / CSM membrane is a positive signal) and that doesn't have a branded service network in your city. The cost of a single service visit from a local plumber after the warranty expires — usually ₹1,500–₹2,500 — often erases the price difference versus a major-brand RO that comes with annual AMC.

Frequently asked questions

Do I actually need an RO water purifier in India?

It depends on your input water. If your municipal supply or borewell has TDS above 500 ppm (most of India does), RO is the practical choice. Below 300 ppm, a UV-only purifier works and keeps minerals in the water. RO strips out everything — including the minerals that make water taste good — which is why TDS controllers and copper / mineral cartridges exist.

What is the recovery rate and why does it matter?

Recovery rate is the percentage of input water that becomes purified water. A 75% recovery RO turns 4 L of input into 3 L of purified water and wastes 1 L. A 50% recovery RO turns 4 L into 2 L and wastes 2 L. In water-scarce cities — Chennai, Bengaluru, parts of Mumbai — the higher recovery rate is worth a small premium. In areas with cheap piped water, the recovery rate is mostly a billing question.

How often do I need to change the filters?

For a typical Indian household (20–30 L/day), the sediment and carbon pre-filters need changing every 6 months, the RO membrane every 12–18 months, and the UV lamp every 12 months. The TDS controller and post-carbon filter usually run 12–18 months. Most brands offer annual maintenance contracts (AMC) at ₹1,500–₹3,000 that cover all of this plus a free service visit.

Is the storage tank of 7–9 L really enough?

For a family of 4–5, 8 L is the practical minimum. RO purifiers produce water slowly — usually 8–15 L per hour — so the tank is what you drink from during the day. Purely tankless RO units exist (they connect directly to a tap) but they have a much lower flow rate and aren't practical for Indian cooking volumes. If you have a large family or cook a lot of dal and rice, go for 9 L or larger.

Can I install an RO purifier myself?

Technically yes, but most brands require authorised installation for the warranty to hold. The install takes 30–60 minutes and typically involves a water-supply tap connector, a drain line, and a wall mount. The first service visit is usually free with a new purchase; the AMC kicks in from year 2.
