What actually matters in a phone under ₹25,000

The mid-range has changed completely in two years. A phone under ₹25,000 in 2026 gives you a 120Hz AMOLED, a 5,000mAh+ battery, 67W+ fast charging and a 50MP main camera with OIS — features that were flagship-exclusive in 2022. The risk is no longer bad hardware; it is choosing a model with poor software support, weak cooling, or a service network that does not cover your city.

For daily use, prioritise the chipset, display and battery before cameras. Snapdragon 7s Gen 2, Dimensity 8200, Dimensity 8300 and Exynos 1380 are the practical floor — they handle social media, BGMI, and on-device AI features without thermal throttling. AMOLED is non-negotiable at this price: LCD only appears in the cheapest sub-₹15,000 phones now.

Software support matters more than people realise. Samsung offers 4 years of OS + 5 years of security on the M35. OnePlus, Nothing and Realme offer 2-3 years. If you keep a phone for 3+ years, a Samsung Galaxy A or M, or a Pixel A-series, is the safer long-term bet even if its camera trails the Chinese competition at launch.

If you only buy one thing from this list: choose the OnePlus Nord CE 4 Lite 5G (8/256GB) when it dips under ₹23,000 on Amazon or OnePlus.in. Its 120Hz AMOLED, 5,500mAh battery and 80W SUPERVOOC charging cover the three things that matter most in a mid-range phone: smooth daily use, all-day battery, and a top-up that finishes in 30 minutes. Three years of OxygenOS updates are the closing argument.

The chipset cheat sheet

At ₹25,000, you will see Snapdragon 695, 6 Gen 1, 7s Gen 2, Dimensity 7050, 7300, 8200, 8300, and Exynos 1380. For everyday use all are fine, but the Dimensity 8300 and Snapdragon 7+ Gen 3 are the real performers — they approach last year's flagship Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 in benchmarks. If you game, prioritise cooling: phones with a stated vapour-chamber area over 3,500 mm² sustain performance longer.

AMOLED vs pOLED and why refresh rate is not everything

AMOLED and pOLED are both OLED; the difference is the substrate (rigid vs flexible) and pOLED allows slightly thinner bezels. Refresh rate (90Hz vs 120Hz vs 144Hz) is noticeable when scrolling but mostly indistinguishable in video. Peak brightness above 1,800 nits is what separates a phone you can read in direct sunlight from one you cannot. Touch sampling rate matters for gaming: 240Hz+ is the gaming-friendly spec.

Charging math that actually matters

67W charging takes a 5,000mAh battery from 0 to ~80% in 30 minutes, and 100% in roughly 45 minutes. 80W cuts another 5-7 minutes. Above 100W, gains flatten. What matters more: does the included charger reach those speeds, or do you need a separate PPS-compatible brick? Many phones now ship with a full-speed charger in the box, but verify before assuming.

Frequently asked questions

Is a phone under ₹25,000 good enough for gaming?

Yes. Snapdragon 7s Gen 2, Dimensity 8200, Dimensity 8300 and Exynos 1380 chipsets handle BGMI, Genshin Impact and on-device AI features at medium-to-high settings. For sustained performance look for a vapour-chamber cooling system over 3,500 mm² and at least 8GB of RAM.

Should I get AMOLED or LCD in this range?

AMOLED is the practical default at ₹25,000. You get true blacks, better outdoor visibility on 1,800+ nit panels, and lower battery drain in dark mode. LCD only makes sense for the cheapest phones under ₹15,000.

How much RAM is enough?

8GB is the minimum in 2026, 12GB is comfortable for heavy multitasking. Storage: 128GB is the floor, 256GB is worth the premium if you shoot a lot of video. Many 256GB variants are only ₹1,500 more than 128GB.

Are mid-range phones good for photography?

Mid-range phones with a 50MP Sony IMX882/IMX896 or Samsung GN8 main sensor and OIS take genuinely good daylight and low-light photos. Telephoto is rare in this range; expect a competent 2MP macro that you will rarely use.

How long will software updates last?

Samsung offers 4 years of OS + 5 years of security on the M35. OnePlus, Nothing, Motorola and Realme offer 2-3 years. If long-term updates matter, Samsung and Pixel A-series are the safer picks.
