What actually matters in a UV water purifier

For Indian households on municipal water with TDS below 500 ppm, a UV purifier is usually a better buy than RO. UV-C light kills bacteria, viruses and cysts without stripping dissolved minerals, produces no reject water, and runs cheaper over its lifetime. The specification sheet is short and the technology is mature, but the differences between models still matter.

Four things separate a good UV purifier from a bad one: UV-C lamp wattage and replacement interval, sediment+carbon filter life, storage tank capacity and material, and whether the unit has a TDS retain mode or strips minerals by default. Get those right and a ₹7,500 UV purifier runs cleanly for five years. Get them wrong and you are replacing filters every four months or, worse, drinking water that does not actually meet the bacterial standard.

If you only buy one thing from this list: the Eureka Forbes AquaSure Amaze. It is the only true UV purifier on the list that combines an 11 W UV-C lamp with a 6,000-litre composite filter, a food-grade ABS tank, and zero reject water — the four specifications that matter most, at a price that lands well under ₹8,500.

UV-C lamp wattage: 11 W is the floor

UV-C dose is the product of lamp wattage and water contact time. A 4-6 W lamp in a cheap unit may glow visibly but delivers a dose well below the 40 mJ/cm² needed to inactivate bacteria and viruses reliably. The picks on this list all ship 8 W or 11 W lamps, which deliver a dose of 30-40 mJ/cm² even at peak flow rates.

The other variable is contact time. A tall, narrow chamber forces water past the lamp slowly; a wide, flat chamber lets water zip past with too little dose. Service-call reviews consistently flag the wider chambers on cheaper units as a problem — they pass the marketing spec on paper but fail the bacterial standard in real use.

Filter life: 6,000 litres is the new baseline

A sediment+carbon pre-filter protects the UV chamber from silt and chlorine, both of which dampen the UV dose. 6,000-litre filter life is the realistic baseline for a metro municipal supply; 1,500-litre units force replacements every four months in most households. Filter cost over five years is where most of the operating expense lives — a cheap unit with a 1,500-litre filter ends up costing more than a mid-range unit with a 6,000-litre one.

Storage tank capacity is the second filter-life concern. Water that sits in a tank longer than 24 hours begins to re-grow bacteria, which makes the filter work harder. A 5-7 litre tank matches a typical urban family of four; anything larger invites stagnation.

Tank material: ABS or stainless, never bare polypropylene

Food-grade ABS and stainless steel tanks resist biofilm formation and are easy to clean. Polypropylene (PP) tanks on budget units are porous at the microscopic level, which lets bacteria colonise the wall and shed back into the water. The picks on this list all use ABS; the skip section calls out PP tanks explicitly.

UV purifier maintenance is straightforward: replace the UV lamp every 12 months and the sediment+carbon filter every 6,000 litres or 12 months, whichever comes first. Most modern units have indicators for both. The cheap units do not, which is why they made the skip list — you have no way to know when the dose has dropped or the filter is saturated.

Frequently asked questions

Is UV water purification enough without RO in India?

Yes for municipal water with TDS below 500 ppm. UV-C light at 254 nm breaks the DNA of bacteria, viruses and cysts, including E. coli, hepatitis A and rotavirus. It does not remove dissolved solids, heavy metals or pesticides — if your source water has those concerns, you need RO first. For most Indian metro households on BWSSB, Delhi Jal Board or municipal corporation supply, UV alone is enough because the dissolved-solid load is already low.

How often does the UV lamp need replacement?

Every 12 months or 6,000 litres, whichever comes first. UV-C output drops sharply after 8,000 hours of use even when the lamp still glows visibly, and bacteria kill rate falls from 99.9 per cent to 70-80 per cent as the lamp ages. Most picks on this list have an indicator light or app notification; the cheap units do not, which is one reason they made the skip list.

Does a UV purifier waste water like RO?

No. A UV purifier does not produce any reject water because it does not remove dissolved solids through a membrane. The only water waste is the small backflush on self-clean cycles, which is well under 5 per cent of inflow. This is the main reason UV is gaining ground in metros where municipal water TDS is already low and water bills are rising.

Can a UV purifier handle borewell or tanker water?

Not reliably. Borewell water in India routinely runs 800-2,000 ppm TDS, and tanker water varies widely. UV light cannot penetrate cloudy water effectively, so heavy sediment blocks the dose. If you have borewell or tanker supply, you need RO+UV, ideally with a pre-sediment filter rated for the local turbidity. Our RO water purifier guide covers the picks worth buying in that scenario.

How long does the storage tank water stay safe?

Up to 24 hours if the tank is food-grade ABS or stainless steel. Beyond 24 hours, bacterial re-growth begins from any spores that survived UV exposure. This is why the picks on this list use ABS or stainless tanks; the cheap units use polypropylene, which is harder to clean and harbours biofilm faster. If your household does not drink 5-7 litres of stored water per day, choose a smaller tank to keep the turnover high.
