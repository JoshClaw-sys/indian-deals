What actually matters in a water purifier under Rs 15,000

A water purifier under Rs 15,000 in India needs to deliver three things: safe drinking water (TDS 50-200, bacteria-free), 100-150 litres/day capacity for a family of 4-5, and low annual maintenance cost (under Rs 3,000/year). Four specs decide which one is worth buying:

  • Purification technology -- RO+UV for high-TDS water (Delhi, Chennai, Hyderabad, Bangalore tap water 500-2000 TDS). UV-only for low-TDS municipal water (Mumbai, Kolkata, parts of Kerala). RO alone wastes 70-80% water; UV alone does not remove dissolved salts.
  • Storage capacity -- 5-7L for families of 2-3. 8-10L for families of 4-5. 12L+ for joint families or office use. Storage matters more than flow rate for Indian usage patterns (morning + evening peak).
  • TDS adjustment -- Required in metros with high-TDS water (500+). A purifier without TDS controller wastes minerals and makes water taste flat. Look for "TDS controller" or "mineralizer" feature.
  • Filter cost -- Annual filter replacement should be under Rs 3,000 for the first 3 years. RO membrane replacement every 2-3 years (Rs 1,500-2,500). UV lamp replacement every 1-2 years (Rs 500-800).

For most Indian homes, an RO+UV purifier with 7-10L storage, TDS controller, and under Rs 3,000/year filter cost is the right shape. The 4 picks below all match.

RO vs UV: which technology do you need

Two distinct technologies for two distinct water problems:

  • RO (Reverse Osmosis) -- Removes dissolved salts, heavy metals (lead, arsenic), fluoride, nitrates. Essential for borewell/tanker water. Wastes 3-4 litres of reject water per 1 litre of purified water. Cannot kill bacteria/viruses by itself -- needs UV or UF backup.
  • UV (Ultraviolet) -- Kills bacteria and viruses using UV light. Essential for municipal water (which often has bacterial contamination). Cannot remove dissolved salts or heavy metals. Wastes no water.

For Indian municipal water (most cities): RO+UV is the safest combination. RO removes dissolved contaminants (industrial waste, old pipes); UV kills bacteria. For very low-TDS water (under 300 TDS, rare in India), UV-only is sufficient. For borewell water (TDS 1000+), RO is non-negotiable.

Frequently asked questions

How much water does an RO purifier waste?

Standard RO purifiers waste 3-4 litres of reject water per 1 litre of purified water (75% rejection rate). Newer "water-saving" RO purifiers (Kent Eco, Pureit Eco) achieve 2:1 ratio -- 2 litres reject per 1 litre purified. The reject water is not dirty -- it is slightly hard water suitable for mopping, gardening, or washing. Do not drink reject water. Use it for plants or cleaning.

What is the best TDS for drinking water?

BIS standard: 100-200 TDS is ideal. Under 100 TDS = flat taste, no minerals. 200-500 TDS = acceptable but tastes hard. Over 500 TDS = unsafe for long-term consumption (kidney stones, hypertension risk). RO purifier output should be 50-150 TDS. The TDS controller blends some raw water back to hit the 100-200 sweet spot. Without TDS controller, RO output is 20-50 TDS (flat taste).

How often should filters be replaced?

Sediment pre-filter: every 6 months (Rs 200-400). Carbon pre-filter: every 12 months (Rs 400-600). RO membrane: every 2-3 years (Rs 1,500-2,500). UV lamp: every 1-2 years (Rs 500-800). Most purifiers have filter-life indicators. Annual filter cost: Rs 2,500-3,500 for the 4 picks above. Without regular filter changes, bacteria grow in the pre-filter and contaminate the purified water.

Can RO water be used for cooking rice and dal?

Yes, but mineral content is lower. RO water is fine for tea, coffee, dal, curry. For chapati dough, RO water sometimes makes the dough feel slimsy (low calcium). If your chapati dough feels off with RO water, blend in 10-20% tap water for the dough only. Do not use reject water for cooking.

Do I need a water purifier if my municipal water is clean?

Even clean municipal water can pick up contaminants in the pipeline to your home (old pipes, biofilm, intermittent contamination during pipe repairs). The 4 picks above provide a safety net. For Mumbai/Kolkata municipal water (low TDS, chlorinated), UV-only is sufficient and saves the RO reject water waste. For Delhi/Chennai/Bangalore/Hyderabad (high TDS, intermittent contamination), RO+UV is essential.

Understanding TDS in Indian water

TDS (Total Dissolved Solids) is the key number for RO/UV decision-making in India:

  • Under 300 TDS -- Soft water, low mineral content. UV-only purifier is sufficient. RO is overkill. Examples: parts of Mumbai, Kerala, Northeast India.
  • 300-600 TDS -- Medium hardness. RO+UV recommended for safety. Most treated municipal water in metros falls here. Examples: Delhi treated water, Bangalore BWSSB water.
  • 600-1500 TDS -- Hard water. RO+UV+UF strongly recommended. Examples: Chennai groundwater, Hyderabad tank water, parts of Delhi.
  • 1500+ TDS -- Very hard water (borewell). RO+UV mandatory. Examples: Rajasthan, Gujarat, parts of Punjab, Haryana.

Test your tap water TDS with a Rs 500-1,000 TDS meter (available on Amazon). The reading determines whether you need RO or UV-only. If TDS is under 300, save the Rs 1,000-2,000 on RO and buy a UV-only purifier like the Eureka Forbes AquaSure (Rs 4,500).

What to do with RO reject water

RO reject water is not sewage -- it is mildly hard water with concentrated minerals. Useful for:

  • Mopping floors -- Minerals actually help clean better than soft water.
  • Washing cars/bikes -- Hard water is fine for vehicle washing.
  • Gardening -- Most plants tolerate hard water; some (azaleas, blueberries) prefer soft water.
  • Toilet flush -- Use a bucket to pour reject water into the cistern. Saves 30-50 litres/day per family of 4.

Divert reject water to a bucket or second storage tank during installation. The plumber can connect the reject pipe to a bucket outlet in 10 minutes for Rs 200-300. Without diversion, reject water flows down the drain and wastes 100+ litres/day per family.
