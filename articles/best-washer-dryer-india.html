What actually matters in a washer dryer combo in India

A washer dryer is two machines in one — a front-load washer plus a built-in condenser or vented dryer. Most Indian homes don't need a dryer (sunny climate, plenty of space to hang clothes). But if you live in a high-rise apartment with no balcony, monsoon-prone city (Mumbai, Goa, Kerala), or work long hours with little drying time, a combo is the right call.

Five things decide if a washer-dryer is worth buying in India: drying capacity (usually 4-5kg vs 7-8kg wash), dryer type (condenser is ventless and works in any room), cycle time (wash+dry takes 4-6 hours on most combos), energy use (drying is power-hungry), and inverter heat pump (cuts drying power use by 50%). Most people overbuy on drying capacity and underbuy on wash capacity — match the wash to your family size, accept the lower drying capacity.

If you only buy one thing from this list: the LG 8kg/5kg Washer Dryer (FHD1408SWS) is the most reliable combo in India. Direct Drive motor + steam + Wi-Fi + 10-year warranty. Worth the premium.

Washer dryer combo vs separate washer + dryer

This is the real question. Combos are convenient but they have a real cost — they do two jobs with one drum, motor, and electronics. Here's how the tradeoffs actually play out in India:

  • Combo pros — Single machine (saves ~6 sq ft floor space), single plumbing, no transferring wet clothes between machines. Important for small apartments.
  • Combo cons — Lower drying capacity (50-60% of wash), longer cycles (4-6 hours), higher repair cost when the dryer breaks (you're without the washer too), uses more electricity (condenser dryers are power-hungry).
  • Separate pros — Larger dryer (matches washer capacity), faster drying (1-2 hours vs 4-6), can run wash and dry simultaneously, easier to service.
  • Separate cons — Costs 30-40% more, takes double the floor space, two plumbing connections, two machines to maintain.

For most Indian apartments (under 800 sq ft), a combo is the practical choice. If you have the space and the budget, separate machines last longer and dry faster.

Frequently asked questions

Is a washer dryer worth it in India?

Worth it if you live in a high-rise apartment without balcony space, monsoon-prone city, or have a small family. Skip if you have a balcony, large family (you'll need a separate dryer for capacity), or live in a dry climate (sun drying is free).

How much does it cost to run a washer dryer?

Washer cycle: ₹8-15 per load (5-star rated, 7-8kg load). Dryer cycle: ₹25-50 per load for condenser dryers, ₹15-25 for heat pump dryers. A typical week (5 wash loads + 4 dry loads) costs ₹200-300 in electricity.

What's the difference between condenser and heat pump dryers?

Condenser dryers use a heating element to evaporate water — faster but power-hungry. Heat pump dryers recycle hot air — 50% less power but slower cycles. For Indian electricity costs, heat pump pays for itself in 3-4 years.

Can I install a washer dryer anywhere in my apartment?

Condenser dryers: yes, ventless, drain into the wash basin or sink. Heat pump dryers: same. Vented dryers: need a wall vent, rarely used in Indian apartments. Combos are louder than washers — avoid installing next to bedrooms.

How long do washer dryers last?

10-12 years for LG/Samsung (Direct Drive motors). 8-10 years for IFB/Bosch. 5-8 years for cheaper brands. The dryer is the failure point — condenser dryers are more failure-prone than heat pump dryers.

The drying capacity math most buyers miss

Almost everyone buys a combo based on wash capacity alone. Then the first time they try to dry a full load, they discover the dryer is rated at 50-60% of wash capacity. A "7kg combo" dries 3.5-4kg per cycle. A full family wash load (5-6kg dry clothes) needs 2 drying cycles.

Three rules to avoid this trap:

  • Buy the largest drying capacity your budget allows — even 1kg more makes a real difference. JBL's 6kg dry and Samsung's 6kg dry are the largest in this segment.
  • Plan for 2 cycles for a full family wash — 8kg wash typically needs 2 dryer cycles of 4kg each. The first cycle is the longest. The second cycle (already-dry clothes) takes 1-2 hours.
  • Use spin-dry before the dryer — 1400 RPM spin removes 50% of water. The dryer then takes 50% less time and uses 30% less power. Always run full spin before the dryer cycle.

The cheapest way to handle this is to use the dryer for "finish drying" only — spin-dry clothes outside, then use the dryer for 30 minutes to remove remaining dampness. This is the most efficient pattern for Indian conditions.

Combos use significantly more electricity than washers alone. A typical Indian household running 5 wash cycles and 3 dry cycles per week will see an additional ₹200-300 in monthly electricity bills. Installation and water requirements

Combos need both a hot and cold water supply — most Indian homes only have cold. The dryer pulls ambient air and heats it, so no external venting is needed. But you do need:

  • Standard 3/4-inch water inlet — same as washers. Most Indian homes have this.
  • Drainage within 1.5m — drain hose into wash basin or floor drain. If your drain is further, you need a drain pump extension (₹800-1,200 accessory).
  • Power outlet within 1m — combos use a 15A plug (not the standard 6A). Most Indian homes have a 15A socket near the washing machine. If not, an electrician needs to run a dedicated line.
  • Stable, level floor — combos weigh 60-80kg. The floor must support this. Wood floors may need reinforcement. Top-loaders are slightly lighter.
  • 2-3 inches clearance on all sides — for ventilation and vibration. Avoid tight closets.

Most brands include installation in the price. Verify with the seller before ordering. If you live in an apartment, building management may have specific installation rules — check first.
