What actually matters in a phone under ₹10,000

Six specs decide if a sub-₹10K phone is worth your money: AMOLED display (not LCD), 6GB+ RAM (4GB is sluggish), 128GB storage (not 64GB), 5G support on SA + NSA bands, 5000mAh battery with 30W+ charging, and a 50MP+ main camera with decent low-light performance. Everything else is secondary.

We compared eight phones available in India in August 2026 in the ₹7,500–₹10,000 bracket — AnTuTu v10 benchmarks, display brightness measurements, camera samples in daylight + night, battery drain tests (video playback + gaming), charging time 0-100%, and software update commitments. Here's what we found — and one popular model with a misleading spec sheet.

If you only buy one thing from this list: get the Redmi Note 13 5G. The 6.67-inch 120Hz AMOLED display, 108MP camera, and 33W fast charging punch well above ₹10K. It's the phone to beat in this segment.

The one we recommend skipping

⚠️ Skip

Itel P55 5G (6GB + 128GB)

₹7,799
ChipsetMediaTek Dimensity 6080 (6nm)
RAM + Storage6GB + 128GB
Camera50MP main + 8MP selfie
Display6.6" HD+ IPS LCD, 60Hz, 500 nits peak

The Itel P55 5G looks attractive at ₹7,799 — same Dimensity 6080 chip as the Redmi Note 13 — but it's a trap. The display is 60Hz (not 120Hz), HD+ resolution (720p, not 1080p), and only 500 nits. Photos from the 50MP camera are washed out — the sensor is a low-end Samsung ISOCELL that struggles in low light.

Itel is a Transsion brand (same parent as Tecno, Infinix) — service network is limited outside metro cities. Software updates are sparse (1 OS update + 2 years security patches at best). The build quality is plasticky and the fingerprint sensor is sluggish.

For ₹2,200 more, the Redmi Note 13 5G is leagues better — AMOLED, 108MP, 33W charging, IP54. For ₹500 more, the Poco M6 Pro gives you Snapdragon + clean HyperOS. The Itel is the cheapest phone on this list for a reason: it's a 2024 budget phone masquerading as a 2026 deal.

It'll work — but you'll feel the compromises within 3 months. Spend the extra ₹2,000-2,500 and get a real budget phone.

The bottom line

For most buyers, the Redmi Note 13 5G is the right choice — best display, best camera, fastest charging. For long-term software support, the Samsung Galaxy M15 5G is the pick (4 OS updates vs 2). For pure budget value, the Poco M6 Pro 5G at ₹8,499 gives you Snapdragon + clean HyperOS.

If you want the 108MP camera without AMOLED, the Realme 12 5G at ₹9,799 works. If you want stock Android + 8GB RAM, the Motorola Moto G34 5G is the only option in this list.

Skip the Itel P55 5G. The savings aren't worth the downgrades.
