What actually matters in a robot vacuum in India

A robot vacuum is a real time-saver for working professionals and pet owners. Five things decide if it's worth buying: suction power (3000+ Pa for Indian dust), navigation (LiDAR > camera > bump-and-go), mopping (yes for Indian homes — daily dust is sticky), app control (must-have), and self-empty station (nice-to-have). Anything with bump-and-go navigation will frustrate you within a week.

For Indian homes, a LiDAR-navigated robot with mopping is the right shape. Indian dust mixes with humidity and becomes sticky — pure vacuum robots don't handle it. Mopping is essential. LiDAR navigation works in the dark (useful for night runs when you're not home).

If you only buy one thing from this list: the Roborock Q7 Max+ for the best LiDAR + mopping combo at this price. The self-empty station is a real time-saver.

Robot vacuum vs cordless stick: which to buy

These solve different problems. Pick based on your use case:

  • Robot vacuum — Runs daily while you're at work, handles the entire floor on a schedule, mop and vacuum in one pass. Best for working professionals, pet owners, large homes. Downside: poor edge cleaning, struggles with thick carpets, can get stuck on cables and rugs.
  • Cordless stick — On-demand, handles stairs, sofa, car interiors, edges. Best for small apartments, quick daily cleans, multi-surface use. Downside: requires you to actually vacuum (you still have to do the work).

Many Indian households own both — the robot handles daily floor cleaning, the stick handles sofa, car, stairs, and edge work. This is the right setup for ₹15,000-20,000 total.

Frequently asked questions

Do robot vacuums work on Indian marble floors?

Yes, all 4 picks work on marble, tile, and wooden floors. Polished marble can confuse cliff sensors (the robot thinks it's an edge) — cover the cliff sensors with tape if this happens, or use a robot with anti-cliff logic (Roborock, iRobot).

Can robot vacuums handle Indian dust and pet hair?

Yes, if suction is 2000+ Pa. Indian dust mixes with humidity and becomes sticky — mopping is essential. All 4 picks have mopping. Pet hair: iRobot's dual rubber brushes are best, then Roborock, then Mi and ECOVACS.

Do they work at night when I'm not home?

Yes, LiDAR navigation works in complete darkness. Schedule the robot to run while you're at work. Most Indian homes benefit from a daily 10am run (after the morning dust settles, before the afternoon humidity).

How often do I need to empty the bin?

Without self-empty: every 2-3 days for 0.4-0.5L bins. With self-empty: every 6-8 weeks. Self-empty stations cost ₹8-12k extra but are worth it for working professionals.

What about mop water refills?

Refill the water tank every 1-2 runs. Mi and ECOVACS have small tanks. Roborock and iRobot have larger tanks. Some models support auto water refill via plumbing (₹50k+) but for most Indian homes, manual refill is fine.

LiDAR vs camera vs bump-and-go: real-world difference

Navigation is the single biggest predictor of whether you'll keep using the robot. Three types, very different experiences:

  • LiDAR — Laser-based mapping. Spins a laser to scan the room 5-10x per second. Creates an accurate floor map. Works in complete darkness. Avoids most obstacles. Best for systematic, complete coverage. The 4 picks in this article all have LiDAR.
  • Camera (vSLAM) — Camera-based mapping. Uses visual landmarks to navigate. Struggles in dark or low light. Can identify specific objects (cables, pet waste). Good for daytime runs.
  • Bump-and-go (gyroscope/random) — No real mapping. Drives until it hits something, then turns randomly. Misses 30-50% of floor area. Common in budget robots (under ₹10,000). Avoid.

For Indian homes with mixed lighting, LiDAR is the best. The 4 picks above all use LiDAR and the difference vs camera robots is significant in dim rooms (early morning, evening runs when lights are off).

The truth about robot mopping

Most robot mops don't really mop — they drag a wet cloth across the floor. Three tiers:

  • Basic mop (gravity water feed) — Cloths drags across floor with constant drip. Decent for daily dust but doesn't scrub stains. Mi Robot Mop 2 Pro, Roborock Q7 Max+ (basic mode).
  • Electronic mop (variable water levels) — Electric pump controls water flow to specific levels (low/medium/high). Better for sticky Indian dust. Roborock Q7 Max+ (advanced mode), ECOVACS Deebot.
  • Vibrating/spinning mop (active scrubbing) — Mop pad vibrates or spins to actively scrub. Best for dried stains. Premium robots (₹40k+).

For Indian homes, electronic mopping (Roborock, ECOVACS) is the sweet spot. Basic gravity mops work for daily dust but struggle with sticky humidity residue.

Tips for setting up a robot vacuum in an Indian home

  • Pick a charging dock with clearance — 1.5m clearance in front, 0.5m on sides. Avoid corners with cable mess. Most Indian homes have one good spot near a wall outlet.
  • Tidy up cables before first run — Robots eat charging cables, earphones, and small toys. Most LiDAR robots can mark "no-go" zones in the app.
  • Schedule for 10am daily — After morning dust settles, before afternoon humidity peaks. Let it run while you're at work.
  • Mark rugs and carpets in the app — Robots try to climb rugs and get stuck. Most apps let you set virtual walls.
  • Clean the dust bin weekly — Even with self-empty, the small bin inside the robot needs weekly manual cleaning.

Most Indian homes benefit from running the robot 5-6 days/week. The daily dust load is significant — once-a-week runs leave visible buildup.
