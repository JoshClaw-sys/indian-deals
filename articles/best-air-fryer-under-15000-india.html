What actually matters in an air fryer under ₹15,000

Air fryers in the ₹8,500–₹15,000 band are the family-size sweet spot in India — large enough to cook for four to six people, fast enough that a 12-minute tikka batch fits a working-week dinner, and reliable enough that the basket survives two to three years of daily use. The specification sheets are close across the band, but the real-world differences are large.

Four things separate a good family-size air fryer from a poor one: basket capacity in litres, wattage and air-circulation technology, temperature range, and whether the basket is dishwasher-safe. Get those right and a ₹12,000 air fryer will run daily for five years. Get them wrong and you have a 4-litre basket that needs two batches for every meal, or a non-stick coating that wears off within a year.

If you only buy one thing from this list: the Philips HD9200/90 (6.2 L). It is the only air fryer under ₹15,000 that pairs 1,425 W rapid-air with a 6.2 L basket, an 80–200°C range and a dishwasher-safe basket — the four specifications that matter most, with the most even cooking in this price band.

Capacity: 6 L is the family-size floor

Indian family cooking is batch-heavy. A typical meal of tikka, fries and roasted vegetables needs at least 4-5 litres of basket space for a family of four. A 3.5-4 L basket forces two batches, adds 15-20 minutes and means the second batch goes out cold by the time the first is eaten. The picks on this list all ship 5.7-6.2 L baskets, which fit a full meal in a single cycle.

Capacity above 6 L is rarely useful unless you regularly cook for six or more. Anything above 8 L physically dominates the kitchen counter and the wattage jumps to 1,700-1,800 W, which burns the surface before the centre cooks.

Wattage and air-circulation: rapid-air wins

Wattage alone is misleading. A 1,500 W unit with a weak fan can cook more slowly than a 1,400 W unit with rapid-air technology. The Philips and Instant picks use propeller-shaped fans that circulate hot air faster than the basic cross-flow fans on cheaper units at the same wattage. The practical difference is 2-4 minutes per batch and noticeably more even browning.

Temperature range is the second variable. A unit that runs 80–200°C handles every Indian use case — dry-roasting at 80°C, baking at 160°C, tikka at 180°C, and searing at 200°C. Cheaper units that top out at 180°C struggle to brown samosas and tikkis, which is the gap between a unit you use weekly and one that sits on the shelf after a month.

Dishwasher-safe baskets: non-negotiable for Indian cooking

Indian marinades — tikka, tandoori, gosht — leave residue that hand-washing struggles to remove from a non-stick coating. Dishwasher-safe baskets use a harder coating that survives 2-3 years of weekly dishwasher cycles. Hand-washing a non-stick basket wears the coating off within a year, and a flaking non-stick surface is a health and cooking problem.

The picks on this list are all dishwasher-safe; the skip section calls out cheaper units with non-stick coatings that do not survive a year of weekly use. If you cannot run a dishwasher, soak the basket in warm water with a drop of dish soap for 10 minutes before hand-washing — it extends coating life by 6-12 months.

Frequently asked questions

How big should the basket be for an Indian family?

At least 5.5 litres for a family of four, and 6 litres or more if you regularly cook for five or more. A 3.5-4 litre basket forces two batches for a single meal of fries and tikka, which adds 15-20 minutes and is the single most common regret in Indian air-fryer buying. The picks on this list all ship 5.7-6.2 L baskets.

Is 1,500 W better than 1,400 W for an air fryer?

The difference between 1,400 and 1,500 W is small. What matters more is the air-circulation technology — the Philips and Instant units move hot air fast enough that the surface crisps in 12-14 minutes, while cheaper 1,500 W units with weaker fans take 16-18 minutes for the same result. Wattage above 1,700 W is rarely useful and can burn the surface before the centre cooks.

Can I cook Indian snacks like tikka and samosas in an air fryer?

Yes, and this is the main reason air fryers have taken off in India. Tikka pieces, paneer tikka, fish tikka and samosas all cook well at 180-200°C for 12-15 minutes with a single flip halfway through. The basket should be preheated for 3 minutes before adding food, and a light brush of oil on tikka pieces improves browning. The 6 L baskets on the picks here comfortably fit 12-15 tikka pieces in a single batch.

Are dishwasher-safe baskets really necessary?

Strongly recommended, especially with Indian cooking. Marinades like tikka and tandoori paste leave residue that hand-washing struggles to remove from a non-stick coating. Dishwasher-safe baskets last 2-3 years longer than the same model hand-washed. The picks on this list are all dishwasher-safe; the skip section calls out cheaper units with non-stick coatings that wear off within a year.

How long do air fryers last?

Five to seven years for the picks on this list, assuming the basket and heating element are cleaned monthly and the basket is not run through the dishwasher more than once a week. The most common failure modes are the non-stick coating wearing off (after 2-3 years of heavy use) and the heating element burning out (after 4-5 years). Philips and Instant offer 2-year warranties in India; Pigeon and Havells offer 1 year.
